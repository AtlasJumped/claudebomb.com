Example answer

Written by Claude from this prompt and a made-up example. Your answer will differ.

See the example

What we gave it: Question: Do standing desks improve the health of office workers? Context: my team asked for them and I need to decide whether to spend part of our budget on them. (Example run without web search.)

No web search in this run. Working only from what I know, which stops at my training cutoff.

Answer
Sit stand desks reliably cut sitting time, but good evidence that they improve health outcomes like weight, blood pressure or back pain is thin. Confidence: moderate.

Sub-questions
1. Do they reduce sitting?
2. Does less sitting improve health?
3. Is standing itself healthy?
4. Do people keep using them?

Key claims
| Claim | Grade | Source |
| Sit stand desks reduce sitting at work in the short term | Moderate | Cochrane review on workplace sitting interventions, 2018 |
| Long sitting is linked to worse health | Moderate | WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour, 2020 |
| Standing all day is not better than sitting | Weak | Mixed studies; I cannot name a single settled source |
| Use drops after a few months | Weak | Small follow-up studies |

Known, likely, unknown
Known: they reduce sitting.
Likely: moving more matters more than standing still.
Unknown: long term health effects.

What would change the answer
A large trial of 12 months or more showing health gains.

Best sources to read
1. The 2018 Cochrane review on reducing sitting at work.
2. The WHO 2020 physical activity guidelines.
I am not sure of other specific sources, so I leave them out. A recent systematic review of long term trials would settle the point.
