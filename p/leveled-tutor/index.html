Example answer

Written by Claude from this prompt and a made-up example. Your answer will differ.

See the example

What we gave it: Subject: basic music theory. Goal: read chord charts and play along with friends on guitar. Time: 15 minutes a day. (Placement test already taken: got questions 1 to 3 right on note names and the musical alphabet, missed 4 to 8 on intervals, keys and chord numbers.)

Your level: you know the musical alphabet and note names, but not yet how notes build into scales and chords.

Your learning map
1. Sharps and flats
2. Half steps and whole steps on the fretboard
3. The major scale pattern
4. The C major scale
5. Keys: what "in the key of G" means
6. Major and minor chords: how they are built
7. The chords that live in one key
8. Chord numbers (I, IV, V)
9. Reading a chord chart: bars and slashes
10. The I IV V progression in three keys
11. The vi chord and common pop progressions
12. Changing key on the spot (transposing)

Does this goal and map look right? Say yes, or tell me what to change.

When you say yes, here is skill 1, starting with a puzzle:

On a piano, between C and D there is a black key. Between E and F there is none. What do you think that black key is called, and why might E and F be different?

Take a guess before I explain.
