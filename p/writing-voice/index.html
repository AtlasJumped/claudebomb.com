Example answer

Written by Claude from this prompt and a made-up example. Your answer will differ.

See the example

What we gave it: My samples (private details removed): 1. "Hi Sam, quick one: can you send the deck by Thurs? Thanks!" 2. "Ha, love it. Count me in." 3. "Hi all, I can't make Friday, sorry. Could we push to Monday? Best, Lena" 4. "No, I'll pass this time, but thanks for thinking of me." 5. A two-paragraph project update with short sentences and no emoji.

Your style profile (from your 5 samples)

Greetings and sign-offs
Work: "Hi Sam," or "Hi all," and "Best, Lena". Friends: no greeting.

Length
Short sentences, often under 12 words. Messages of one to three lines. Updates in two short paragraphs.

Tone
Friendly and direct: "quick one: can you send the deck by Thurs?"
Light humor with friends: "Ha, love it."
Polite but brief.

Words you use
"quick one", "Thanks!", "Count me in", "sorry", abbreviations like "Thurs".

Punctuation
One exclamation mark at most per message. Colons to get to the point. No emoji in these samples.

Asking, saying no, apologizing
Ask with "can you" or "could we" plus a date.
Say no plainly, then thank: "No, I'll pass this time, but thanks for thinking of me."
Apologize in one word: "sorry".

Test drafts
Friendly reply: "Ha, great news. Count me in for Saturday!"
Firm no: "No, I can't take this on this month, but thanks for asking."
Request to someone new: "Hi Dana, quick one: could you share the venue floor plan by Fri? Thanks, Lena"

What sounds wrong? Tell me and I will update the profile.

Final profile (to paste)
When drafting anything in my name, follow this style profile.
Short sentences, one to three lines. Open work notes with "Hi [name]," and sign "Best, Lena". Get to the point fast, often with a colon. Ask with "can you" or "could we" plus a date. Say no plainly, then thank. One exclamation mark at most. No emoji.
