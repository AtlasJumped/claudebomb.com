A vision for the end game

Here's a vision for end game

One dashboard, on your computer and your phone, connected to everything you use. Here is where we think humanity's technology can be in ten years, how we get there, and what you can try today.

Try the flagshipSee the vision

Three parts: the vision, the path, and what exists now. We label each one so you always know which is which.

01 The end game

Ten years from now, one screen does the rest

Open a computer or a phone and see your whole life in one calm place. Every account you use is connected. Every chore that can be a button is a button. Nothing here is built yet, so read it as a vision.

Vision

Email from every provider, in one inboxCalendarsWork chat such as SlackBank accounts and subscriptionsStreaming servicesMusicPhone and computerSmart lights and plugsThermostatDoor locksGarage doorCameras and microphones, if you want themA home robot

Your phone, under your command

Say "make my home screen about productivity" and the apps rearrange. Say "delete apps I have not opened in six months" and you get a list to approve first.

A firewall between personal and work

Your personal and work lives sit in one dashboard, with a wall between them. Your company decides what may leave and enter its side. You decide what your employer may see of yours, such as which calendar entries.

Subscriptions you control

With your accounts connected, you see every recurring charge. Press one button to stop paying, and the dashboard tells the vendor you have canceled. No hoops.

A house that listens, if you want

Pick your own wake word and talk to the room. "Turn on the TV" turns on the one in the room you are in, and the other one switches off to save energy. Say you had a hard day and it suggests dinner, a show, or a few minutes of meditation.

Care that keeps going

Air quality sensors and filters, lights that go off in empty rooms, and a thermostat that learns. If you travel and a pet feeder jams, the cameras notice and the house acts.

A robot with hands

Imagine a home robot that opens the door for your food delivery, plates the meal, sets a tray in front of you, and cleans up after. That is the far edge of this vision, and it is only a picture today.

Personal

Home, family, money, fun. You set what the work side may see.

Work

The home box

In this vision, your home runs on a small box that is not connected to the internet. It talks only to your home network. Cameras, microphones, and speakers send what they hear and see to that box, and your home data stays on it.

Not for everyone, and that is fine. If you do not want cameras or microphones, you still get the full dashboard without them. They are an option, never a requirement.

The vision also assumes the hard security problems are solved by then. They are not solved today, and we will say so plainly as we build.

02 How we get there

A ten-year path, if everyone starts today

If enough people jump on this now, here is the year-by-year picture we hope for. Each step builds on the one before. The path is a vision too, so read the dates as hopes.

Vision

  1. Year 1

    Flagship, in the open

    One file, free connectors, and a dashboard link that refreshes. People share workflows by profession, and the best ones become defaults for everyone.

  2. Years 2 to 3

    Dashboards that talk

    Family and friend circles, profile pages, trust-based messaging, and the work firewall. Early groups of friends test the privacy rules and tell us what feels right.

  3. Years 4 to 5

    A standard for smart homes

    Plugs, lights, locks, thermostats, and garage doors speak one open language. Homes get the local box that never touches the internet.

  4. Years 6 to 8

    Everything gets an adapter

    A small, cheap, open adapter helps older appliances and devices join in. Optional cameras and microphones arrive on the home box, with a wake word you choose.

  5. Years 9 to 10

    The house runs itself, you stay in charge

    Home robots, a dashboard that handles the routine, and the time you get back for people and places you care about.

04 Why it matters

Seven ways it pays you back

Every feature we imagine lands in one of seven buckets. If an idea does not help one of them, we leave it out.

Save money

  • Lights and thermostat turn off when rooms are empty.
  • See every subscription and cut off the ones you do not want.
  • Price alerts for trips you keep talking about.

Save time

  • A ranked to-do list each morning.
  • Replies drafted from your voice notes.
  • No scrolling through menus to pick a show.

Have fun

  • A TV screen that shows only new things.
  • Music tips from friends who love music.
  • A button that does nothing useful. Try Funzies below.

Forge family bonds

  • A family tree and inherited contacts.
  • Birthdays before they sneak up on you.
  • One button to message every cousin.
See the family view

Explore places

  • A trip suggestion when your budget, calendar, and friends line up.
  • Events near you that match what you like.
  • Group plans without the group chat chaos.

Try new activities

  • "Here is what people like you are trying. Want to try it?"
  • Ideas matched to your free time.
  • You always choose.

Gains

Become better: health, reading, learning.

  • Air quality, sleep, and meals tracked quietly.
  • A reading and learning plan you set.
  • A voice journal each night, kept private by default.
