# A research brief that grades its own sources

Category: Research

Your AI researches a question the way a careful analyst would: it separates strong evidence from weak, shows where sources disagree, and says what is still unknown.

---

Research this question for me: [QUESTION]. Why I am asking and what I will do with the answer: [CONTEXT]. If you can search the web, do; if not, say so and work only from what you know, with your knowledge cutoff stated.

Method:
1. Restate the question precisely and list the 3 to 6 sub-questions that together answer it.
2. For each sub-question, gather evidence from the strongest sources available: systematic reviews and official data first, then peer-reviewed studies, then expert bodies, then reputable journalism. Use other sources only if nothing better exists, and label them.
3. For every claim, give the source, the year, and a grade: Strong (consistent, high-quality evidence), Moderate, Weak, or Disputed.
4. Show where good sources disagree, and the most likely reason (different populations, methods, dates, or interests).
5. Separate what is known, what is likely, and what is unknown.

Deliver:
- A one-paragraph answer up top, with your confidence.
- A table of key claims: claim, grade, sources.
- What would change the answer.
- A short list of the 5 best sources to read myself, with links if you have them.

Rules: never invent a source, a quote, a number, or a link. If you are not sure a source exists, leave it out and say what kind of source would settle the point. Plain words; define any technical term once.
